# Favorite Problems

A running list of open-ended questions to test new information against as you capture it. Per [[Chapter 4 - Capture - Keep What Resonates|Chapter 4]]: keep a dozen (give or take) consistent, open-ended questions in mind; when something you read, hear, or experience sheds light on one of them, that's a strong signal it's worth capturing. These tend to stay stable across years even as projects and jobs change — revisit and refine this list periodically rather than treating it as fixed.

## My favorite problems (seed list — edit freely)

1. How do I build Gloss Local (and tools like it) so capture is effortless *and* retrieval is actually reliable, not just one or the other?
2. What's the minimum viable local-first stack for a personal tool — where's the line between "simple enough to maintain alone" and "too limited to be useful"?
3. How do I turn what I read into something I actually act on, instead of just accumulating highlights and notes?

## How to use this list
- Don't worry about hitting exactly twelve — a few genuine ones beats a padded list.
- Phrase each as an open-ended question (multiple possible answers), not yes/no.
- When you're deciding whether to capture something (a Gloss Local annotation, a book note, a web clip), test it against this list: does it shed any light on one of these? If yes, that's a strong independent signal alongside "does it resonate."
- Ask people who've known you a long time what you were obsessed with as a kid — Forte's own example (LEGO organization → his adult fascination with organizing digital information) is the pattern to look for.
